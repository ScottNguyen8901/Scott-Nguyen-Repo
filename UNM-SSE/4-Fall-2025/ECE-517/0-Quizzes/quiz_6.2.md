## Question 3  
The Mercer's theorem says that  
Group of answer choices

- **Any positive semidefinite function \( k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \in \mathbb{R} \) is a dot product in a Hilbert space.**  
- If a function \( k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \in \mathbb{R} \) is a kernel, then it must be a positive semidefinite function.  
- **If a positive semidefinite function \( k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \in \mathbb{R} \) exists, then an explicit transformation into a Hilbert space exists.**  
- **If \( k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \in \mathbb{R} \) is positive semidefinite, then we can express a mapping function from it.**

## Question 4  
Two different Mercer's kernels  
Group of answer choices

- **Can be used to construct another kernel by a linear combination of them with positive coefficients**  
- **Can be used to construct another kernel by multiplying them**  
- Can be used to construct another kernel by subtracting one from another.  
- Cannot be used together if one is a kernel of an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and the other is finite.
